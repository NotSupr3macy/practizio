The Cold Start Problem
You're building a marketplace. Businesses won't join unless AI agents are using the directory. AI agents won't use the directory unless businesses are listed. This is the classic chicken-and-egg problem, and every marketplace (Uber, Airbnb, DoorDash) had to solve it the same way: you subsidize one side to attract the other.
In your case, you subsidize the business side. You make it free and effortless to join, and you do the work to bring AI agent traffic to the directory.
Don't Charge Businesses to Get Listed — Charge for Value After They See It
Here's the pricing strategy that actually works for marketplaces at your stage:
Free forever to list and get an MCP endpoint. Every business gets a live MCP endpoint, directory listing, and public profile page at zero cost. No credit card required. No trial period. No feature gating on the core product. This needs to feel like "why wouldn't I do this?" to a business owner. The moment you put a paywall in front of onboarding, your signup rate drops by 80% or more. You have zero leverage right now — no traffic, no proof it works, no case studies. Free is the only move.
Charge later for premium features and volume. Once a business is getting AI-booked appointments and can see the value on their dashboard, THEN you introduce paid tiers. The free tier stays free but with a cap (10 AI bookings per month). Most small businesses won't hit that cap for a while, which is fine — they're still adding to your directory and making the network more valuable. The ones who do hit it are the ones getting real value, and they'll gladly pay $49 or $149 a month because you're literally sending them customers.
The per-booking model is your best friend early on. Instead of asking businesses to pay a flat monthly fee before they've seen results, charge $2-3 per successful AI-booked appointment. The pitch is "you pay nothing until AI actually books you a customer." This is risk-free for them and aligns your incentive with theirs. You only make money when they make money.
